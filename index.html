Marginalia

The reader-marginalia an author would otherwise never see.

When a real reader marks up the margins of a book with their reactions, those notes stay with the reader — the author never gets that view. Marginalia closes that loop: paste your draft into the canvas, hand it to a reader, and see the kind of marginal annotations they would have written. Cross-references, parallels from traditions you haven't cited, naive questions, skeptical pushback.

Heritage

Built with Claude. The system prompt was iterated against Claude's voice and reasoning depth, and the default reader is Claude Haiku 4.5 — that's what the tool was tuned for and where it feels most coherent.

The architecture, however, is model-agnostic. The worker uses a provider-adapter pattern; the Anthropic adapter is the first adapter, not the only code path. OpenAI or any chat-capable LLM can be added in roughly twenty lines. Pick your favorite reader.

How it works

Three pieces:

  • Frontend — vanilla HTML/CSS/JS, no build step. Six fixed margin slots (3 left, 3 right) hold the marginalia. User-summoned only; no auto-refresh on a timer. The user owns the rate of arrival.
  • Worker — Cloudflare Worker that proxies requests to whatever LLM provider you've configured. Holds the API key. Handles CORS. Returns up to 6 marginalia per call.
  • System prompt — casts the model as a reader of the draft, not a commentator or assistant. Explicitly varies the perspectives the marginalia come from (more-knowledgeable, less-knowledgeable, disagreeing, cross-domain). Refuses author-echo by design.

Design principles

  • No drift. Marginalia don't move. Real notes in real books don't drift across the page; the brain treats peripheral motion as interruption regardless of how slow it is. The orbits sit still until you summon a new set.
  • No auto-refresh. Notes get added when a reader has a thought, not on a timer. The mind needs processing time between intellectual stimuli; the user owns the rate.
  • Reader-not-author voice. The system prompt does not instruct the model to "match the author's register." Doing so produces author-echo. Instead it asks the model to vary the origins of the marginalia — sometimes more-knowledgeable, sometimes less, sometimes disagreeing, sometimes connecting to a domain the author hasn't cited.
  • Pin economy. Click a fragment to pin it; the worker is then asked for only the count of fresh fragments needed. Token cost scales with what's actually requested.

Reader stances

Drafting — six probing fragments from varied perspectives. Four reader-types: one who knows more than the author, one who knows less, one who disagrees or notices a weak seam, and one who connects the passage to a completely different domain. The working-draft reader — skeptical, curious, cross-disciplinary.

Reading — three sparse fragments weighted toward recognition (naming a structural move), association (cross-text resonance), and appreciation (the gutter checkmark). The finished-piece reader. If a fragment sounds like an editor, the system rewrites it.

An optional form hint (poetry, essay, fiction, memoir, technical writing, etc.) composes on top of whichever stance is active — same passage produces meaningfully different reads across combinations.

Host your own

  1. Fork the repo.
  2. Deploy the worker: cd worker && npx wrangler secret put ANTHROPIC_API_KEY && npx wrangler deploy
  3. Point WORKER_URL in js/app.js at your deployed worker.
  4. Add your frontend's URL to ALLOWED_ORIGINS in worker/wrangler.toml.
  5. Deploy the frontend — Netlify, Cloudflare Pages, GitHub Pages, anything that serves static files.

Swap providers

The worker uses an adapter pattern. Each adapter is one async function that calls a provider's chat-completion endpoint and returns the model's text response. Five providers ship out of the box — Anthropic, OpenAI, Mistral, Ollama, and a free Workers AI fallback. Add your own by following the pattern in the source.

Iteration history

Marginalia was iterated through seven design passes, each informed by real writing sessions:

  1. Drifting orbits, auto-refresh, matched register — "fidgety, distracting"
  2. Still orbits, auto-refresh — "12 seconds feels chaotic"
  3. Still orbits, user-summoned, matched register — "it's just what I would probably write"
  4. Still, user-summoned, outside-reader voice — landed Phase 1A
  5. Single-stance on creative text — "feels like I dropped my narrative into a Facebook group"
  6. Drafting / reading toggle — landed: "these feel like things people would genuinely write in the margins"
  7. Form-hint axis — landed: drafting + creative writing produced craft critique the tool literally couldn't reach before

Each pivot is documented in the repo. The lessons generalize past this project.

About Marginalia

What it is

Marginalia is a thinking surface for writers. You write in a central canvas; an AI reader marks up the margins — the way Coleridge, Borges, or any thoughtful reader would annotate a book they were genuinely reading.

The thesis is simple: the reader-marginalia an author would otherwise never see. When a real reader marks up the margins of a book, those notes stay with the reader — the author never gets that view. Marginalia closes that loop.

How it works

When you press summon, your text is sent to a language model that reads it as a reader — not an editor, not an assistant. The model generates short marginal fragments (surprise, disagreement, cross-reference, recognition) and returns them. Nothing is stored. Your text is not used for training. Each summon is a single read — the model has no memory between summons.

Two reader stances are available: drafting (six probing fragments from varied perspectives — skeptical, curious, cross-disciplinary) and reading (three sparse fragments weighted toward recognition and appreciation, for finished work). An optional form hint (poetry, essay, fiction, etc.) calibrates what the reader attends to.

What it provides

Writers already know what they think. The next thought is always available. What's harder to access is the reader's experience — the marginal note from someone who knows more about etymology, or less about your subject, or who disagrees with your framing, or who connects your passage to a tradition you haven't cited.

Marginalia surfaces those readings. Not corrections. Not coaching. Not "have you considered." The genuine marginalia of a reader who took the work seriously for what it is.

Architecture

Marginalia is model-agnostic and open source. The default reader is Claude Haiku, but the system is designed to work with any chat-capable language model. A free trial seed lets you try the tool without an API key. After that, bring your own key for any supported provider, or continue with the free open model.

Your API key is stored in your browser only — sent directly to your chosen provider, never logged, never stored on the server. The full source is open for inspection.
